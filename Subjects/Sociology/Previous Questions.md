## Q1. Define the concept of culture, and discuss the main elements of culture with suitable examples. [05]

**Answer.**

**Definition.** Culture is the shared, learned way of life of a society. It includes the knowledge, beliefs, values, norms, customs, language, art, and material objects that people acquire as members of a society. E. B. Tylor defined culture as “that complex whole which includes knowledge, belief, art, morals, law, custom, and any other capabilities and habits acquired by man as a member of society.”

**Main elements of culture:**

1. **Symbols:** anything that carries a shared meaning, such as a flag, a wedding ring, a gesture, or a traffic sign.
2. **Language:** a system of symbols used for communication. It transmits culture from one generation to the next and shapes how people think.
3. **Values:** shared standards about what is good, desirable, and important, such as honesty, freedom, justice, and family loyalty.
4. **Beliefs:** ideas people hold about reality, including religious, scientific, and moral beliefs.
5. **Norms:** rules that guide behavior. They include:
   - **Folkways:** everyday customs (shaking hands, dressing appropriately).
   - **Mores:** strong moral rules (not stealing, not harming others).
   - **Laws:** formal norms enforced by the state.
6. **Customs and traditions:** inherited practices such as festivals, marriage rituals, and food habits.
7. **Material culture:** physical objects and technology, such as tools, buildings, clothing, smartphones, and roads.

**Examples:** In Bangladeshi culture, Eid festivals, Bangla language, respect for elders, and traditional clothing are cultural elements. In engineering, safety standards and professional codes are also cultural norms of the profession.

**Conclusion:** culture is learned, shared, transmitted, symbolic, dynamic, and adaptive. It shapes individual behavior and social life.

## Q2. Explain the etymology and definition of corruption, and describe its major forms with examples. [05]

**Answer.**

**Etymology.** The word “corruption” comes from the Latin **corruptus**, meaning “spoiled” or “destroyed,” and **corrumpere**, meaning “to destroy, spoil, or bribe.” The idea is a perversion or breakdown of integrity, honesty, and proper conduct.

**Definition.** Corruption is the **abuse of entrusted power for private gain**. It involves dishonest or illegal behavior by people in positions of authority, often to obtain money, favors, or unfair advantage.

**Major forms of corruption:**

1. **Bribery:** giving or receiving money, gifts, or favors to influence a decision. Example: paying an official to approve a contract.
2. **Embezzlement:** theft or misuse of money or property entrusted to one’s care. Example: an employee transferring company funds to a personal account.
3. **Nepotism:** favoritism toward relatives in appointments or promotions. Example: hiring an unqualified relative.
4. **Cronyism:** favoritism toward friends or associates. Example: awarding a contract to a friend’s company without competition.
5. **Extortion:** using force or threats to obtain money or favors. Example: demanding payment to provide a public service.
6. **Fraud:** deception for financial or personal gain. Example: false invoices or fake qualifications.
7. **Kickbacks:** illegal payments made in return for favorable treatment. Example: a supplier pays an official for a contract.
8. **Money laundering:** hiding the source of illegal money. Example: moving corrupt funds through fake businesses.
9. **Influence peddling:** selling access or influence to decision-makers.

**Effects:** corruption wastes public resources, weakens institutions, increases inequality, reduces trust, and can endanger public safety when standards are ignored.

**Conclusion:** corruption is not only bribery; it includes many forms of abuse of trust. It damages both organizations and society.

## Q3. Classify the major types of poverty and describe their characteristics with suitable examples. [05]

**Answer.**

**Definition.** Poverty is the condition of lacking the basic necessities of life, such as sufficient income, food, shelter, healthcare, education, and safe water, to maintain a minimum acceptable standard of living.

**Major types of poverty:**

1. **Absolute poverty:** people fall below a fixed subsistence threshold and cannot meet basic needs. Characteristics: hunger, unsafe housing, lack of clean water, poor health. Example: a family unable to afford minimum food and shelter.
2. **Relative poverty:** income is below the average standard of the society, so people cannot participate in normal social life. Characteristics: social exclusion, limited opportunities, comparison with others. Example: a low-income worker in a wealthy city.
3. **Situational or transient poverty:** temporary poverty caused by a shock such as job loss, illness, disaster, or displacement. Characteristics: sudden fall in income; may recover with support. Example: a family affected by a flood.
4. **Generational or chronic poverty:** long-term poverty passed from one generation to the next. Characteristics: low education, unemployment, poor health, lack of assets. Example: a family trapped in poverty for decades.
5. **Urban poverty:** poverty in cities and towns. Characteristics: slums, informal work, high living costs, overcrowding. Example: street vendors living in informal settlements.
6. **Rural poverty:** poverty in rural areas. Characteristics: landlessness, low agricultural productivity, limited services, seasonal income. Example: small farmers with low yields.
7. **Subjective poverty:** people’s own assessment that their income is insufficient for their needs. Characteristics: perceived deprivation; varies by person.

**Common characteristics:** low income, unemployment or precarious work, poor housing, malnutrition, limited education and healthcare, vulnerability to shocks, and social exclusion.

**Conclusion:** poverty is multidimensional. It is not only low income but also a lack of capabilities, opportunities, and security.
