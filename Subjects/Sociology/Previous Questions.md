## Q1. Define the concept of culture, and discuss the main elements of culture with suitable examples. [05]

**Answer.**

**Marking scheme:** definition (2) + five elements with examples (3).

**Definition.** Culture is the shared, learned way of life of a society. It includes the knowledge, beliefs, values, norms, customs, language, art, and material objects that people acquire as members of a society. E. B. Tylor defined culture as “that complex whole which includes knowledge, belief, art, morals, law, custom, and any other capabilities and habits acquired by man as a member of society.”

**Main elements of culture:**

1. **Symbols:** anything that carries a shared meaning. Example: a national flag, a wedding ring, a traffic sign.
2. **Language:** a system of symbols used for communication. Example: Bangla in Bangladesh; language transmits culture across generations and shapes thought.
3. **Values:** shared standards about what is good, desirable, and important. Example: honesty, freedom, justice, family loyalty.
4. **Beliefs:** ideas people hold about reality. Example: religious beliefs, scientific beliefs, moral beliefs.
5. **Norms:** rules that guide behavior.
   - **Folkways:** everyday customs (shaking hands, dressing appropriately).
   - **Mores:** strong moral rules (not stealing, not harming others).
   - **Laws:** formal norms enforced by the state.
6. **Customs and traditions:** inherited practices such as festivals, marriage rituals, and food habits. Example: Eid celebrations, traditional clothing.
7. **Material culture:** physical objects and technology. Example: tools, buildings, clothing, smartphones, roads.

**Examples.** In Bangladeshi culture, Eid festivals, the Bangla language, respect for elders, and traditional clothing are cultural elements. In engineering, safety standards and professional codes are also cultural norms of the profession.

**Conclusion.** Culture is learned, shared, transmitted, symbolic, dynamic, and adaptive. It shapes individual behavior and social life.

**Lecture link:** Sociology Part 2 — Culture and Society; elements of culture, symbols, language, values, norms, folkways, mores, laws.

## Q2. Explain the etymology and definition of corruption, and describe its major forms with examples. [05]

**Answer.**

**Marking scheme:** etymology (1) + definition (2) + four forms with examples (2).

**Etymology.** The word “corruption” comes from the Latin **corruptus**, meaning “spoiled” or “destroyed,” and **corrumpere**, meaning “to destroy, spoil, or bribe.” The idea is a perversion or breakdown of integrity, honesty, and proper conduct.

**Definition.** Corruption is the **abuse of entrusted power for private gain**. It involves dishonest or illegal behavior by people in positions of authority, often to obtain money, favors, or unfair advantage.

**Major forms of corruption:**

1. **Bribery:** giving or receiving money, gifts, or favors to influence a decision. Example: paying an official to approve a contract.
2. **Embezzlement:** theft or misuse of money or property entrusted to one’s care. Example: an employee transferring company funds to a personal account.
3. **Nepotism:** favoritism toward relatives in appointments or promotions. Example: hiring an unqualified relative.
4. **Cronyism:** favoritism toward friends or associates. Example: awarding a contract to a friend’s company without competition.
5. **Extortion:** using force or threats to obtain money or favors. Example: demanding payment to provide a public service.
6. **Fraud:** deception for financial or personal gain. Example: false invoices or fake qualifications.
7. **Kickbacks:** illegal payments made in return for favorable treatment. Example: a supplier pays an official for a contract.
8. **Money laundering:** hiding the source of illegal money. Example: moving corrupt funds through fake businesses.
9. **Influence peddling:** selling access or influence to decision-makers.

*(Any four forms with examples earn full marks.)*

**Effects.** Corruption wastes public resources, weakens institutions, increases inequality, reduces trust, and can endanger public safety when standards are ignored.

**Conclusion.** Corruption is not only bribery; it includes many forms of abuse of trust. It damages both organizations and society.

## Q3. Classify the major types of poverty and describe their characteristics with suitable examples. [05]

**Answer.**

**Marking scheme:** definition (2) + four types with characteristics and examples (3).

**Definition.** Poverty is the condition of lacking the basic necessities of life, such as sufficient income, food, shelter, healthcare, education, and safe water, to maintain a minimum acceptable standard of living.

**Major types of poverty:**

1. **Absolute poverty:** people fall below a fixed subsistence threshold and cannot meet basic needs. Characteristics: hunger, unsafe housing, lack of clean water, poor health. Example: a family unable to afford minimum food and shelter.
2. **Relative poverty:** income is below the average standard of society, so people cannot participate in normal social life. Characteristics: social exclusion, limited opportunities, comparison with others. Example: a low-income worker in a wealthy city.
3. **Situational or transient poverty:** temporary poverty caused by a shock such as job loss, illness, disaster, or displacement. Characteristics: sudden fall in income; may recover with support. Example: a family affected by a flood.
4. **Generational or chronic poverty:** long-term poverty passed from one generation to the next. Characteristics: low education, unemployment, poor health, lack of assets. Example: a family trapped in poverty for decades.
5. **Urban poverty:** poverty in cities and towns. Characteristics: slums, informal work, high living costs, overcrowding. Example: street vendors living in informal settlements.
6. **Rural poverty:** poverty in rural areas. Characteristics: landlessness, low agricultural productivity, limited services, seasonal income. Example: small farmers with low yields.

*(Any four types with characteristics and examples earn full marks.)*

**Common characteristics:** low income, unemployment or precarious work, poor housing, malnutrition, limited education and healthcare, vulnerability to shocks, social exclusion.

**Conclusion.** Poverty is multidimensional. It is not only low income but also a lack of capabilities, opportunities, and security.

### Sociology — Final Fall 2025, GED 421 (page 13)

**Q1.** Discuss “Social Stratification” in the context of Bangladesh and its impact. [20]

**Answer.**

**Marking scheme:** definition (4) + forms/manifestations in Bangladesh (6) + impacts (6) + engineering relevance (4).

**Social stratification** is the hierarchical ranking of people in a society by wealth, power, education, occupation, birth, and gender.

**In Bangladesh,** stratification appears through:
- Landownership (large landowners vs landless farmers)
- Education access (elite English-medium vs public/madrassa schools)
- Urban–rural divides (Dhaka/Chattogram vs rural villages)
- Remittance-based class differences (families with overseas workers vs without)
- Gender discrimination (women’s limited access to education, jobs, leadership)
- Informal vs formal employment (day laborers vs salaried professionals)

**Impacts:**
- Unequal access to health care, education, and jobs
- Limited social mobility (children of poor parents stay poor)
- Concentration of political influence in elite groups
- Social tension, crime, and weaker trust in institutions

**Engineering relevance.** Engineers should design affordable, inclusive technology and infrastructure that serves lower-income and rural users — not only elite groups. Examples: low-cost water purification, off-grid solar for rural clinics, affordable assistive devices, inclusive public-transport design.

**Lecture link:** Sociology Part 4 — Social Change; social inequality; engineering and social responsibility.

### Sociology — Final Spring 2026, GED 421 (page 3)

**Q1.** How does social stratification create discrimination and social problems? Discuss in the context of Bangladesh. [20]

**Answer.**

**Marking scheme:** mechanism of discrimination (6) + Bangladesh context (6) + resulting social problems (6) + responses (2).

Stratification assigns unequal value and opportunity to groups based on class, caste, gender, religion, or location. This causes discrimination in jobs, education, housing, and services. In Bangladesh this deepens poverty, restricts women’s opportunities, marginalizes minorities and ethnic groups, and widens urban–rural gaps.

**Resulting social problems:**
- Crime (linked to poverty and lack of opportunity)
- Poor health and malnutrition among the poor
- Low school completion and child labor
- Weaker trust in institutions and the state

**Responses.** Inclusive public policy, quality public schools and health services, equal-opportunity employment, affirmative action for disadvantaged groups, and engineering solutions designed for low-income users.

**Lecture link:** Sociology Part 4 — Social Change; conflict theory (change results from conflict between groups with unequal power).

### Sociology — Mid Fall 2025, GED 421 (page 11)

**Q1(a).** Define “research” and choose a research topic with social impact. [5+?]

**Q1(b).** Describe a research proposal to conduct the research successfully. [15]

**Q2.** Describe the process and factors of socialization and how they influence personal development and identity formation. [20]

**Answer.**

**(a) Research** is systematic investigation to establish facts, test theories, or solve problems. Example socially relevant topic: “Impact of ride-sharing apps on urban mobility and traffic in Dhaka.”

**(b) Research proposal elements:** title; problem statement and rationale; research objectives; literature review; methodology (survey/interviews, sampling, data collection and analysis); timeline; expected outcomes; ethics (informed consent, privacy, confidentiality).

**(c) Socialization** is the process by which people learn culture, norms, roles, and values. **Agents of socialization:** family, school, peers, media, workplace, religious institutions. It shapes language, behavior, gender roles, identity, and social skills. **Factors:** agents of socialization, culture, social class, education, and life events. For engineers, **professional socialization** adds safety culture, engineering ethics, teamwork, and public-safety responsibility.

**Lecture link:** Sociology Part 1 — Introduction to Sociology; Part 2 — Culture and Society (socialization); Part 4 — Social Change.
