## Part 1 — Introduction to Sociology

- **Video for this topic:** [The Sociological Imagination Explained](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BINK6r1Wy78) — Sociology Live!.
- **Sociology** is the systematic study of society, social institutions, social relationships, and human social behavior.
- It emerged in the 19th century as a response to industrialization, urbanization, and social change.
- **Sociological imagination:** the ability to connect personal troubles to broader social structures and historical forces.
- **Scope:** family, education, religion, economy, politics, culture, crime, inequality, social change.
- **Relevance to engineering:** engineers work within social systems; technology affects people, institutions, safety, and the environment. Sociology helps engineers understand users, organizations, inequality, and the social consequences of technology.
- **Social institutions:** organized patterns of norms and roles that meet social needs, such as family, education, economy, government, and religion.

## Part 2 — Culture and Society

- **Video for this topic:** [Symbols, Values and Norms: Crash Course Sociology #10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kGrVhM_Gi8k) — CrashCourse.
- **Culture:** the shared, learned way of life of a society; includes knowledge, beliefs, values, norms, language, customs, art, and material objects.
- **Elements of culture:**
  - Symbols
  - Language
  - Values
  - Beliefs
  - Norms (folkways, mores, laws)
  - Customs and traditions
  - Material culture/technology
- **Characteristics of culture:** learned, shared, symbolic, transmitted, dynamic, adaptive, and integrated.
- **Society:** a group of people who live together in an organized way and share institutions and culture.
- **Socialization:** the process by which people learn culture, norms, and roles.
- **Cultural diversity:** differences among cultures; avoid **ethnocentrism** (judging another culture by one’s own standards) and consider **cultural relativism**.
- **Culture and engineering:** safety culture, professional norms, communication, design for different users, and the social impact of technology.

## Part 3 — Crime

- **Video for this topic:** [Deviance: Crash Course Sociology #18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BGq9zW9w3Fw) — CrashCourse.

> [!info] Scope
> The teacher suggestion says **Crime only** for Part 3. Do not over-expand into all of deviance unless it supports the crime answer.

- **Crime:** behavior that violates a formal criminal law and is punishable by the state.
- **Deviance:** behavior that violates social norms; not all deviance is crime, and not all crime is considered deviant by everyone.
- **Types of crime:** violent crime, property crime, white-collar crime, organized crime, cybercrime, victimless crime (debated).
- **Causes of crime:** biological, psychological, and sociological explanations.
  - **Strain theory:** blocked opportunities create pressure to achieve goals illegally.
  - **Labeling theory:** being labeled deviant can push people toward criminal behavior.
  - **Social control theory:** weak social bonds reduce conformity.
  - **Differential association:** criminal behavior is learned through interaction.
- **Social control:** formal control (police, courts, prisons) and informal control (family, school, peers, religion).
- **Criminal justice system:** police, courts, corrections.
- **White-collar crime:** financially motivated, nonviolent crime by people in positions of trust; relevant to corruption and engineering ethics.
- **Engineering relevance:** safety violations, fraud, bribery, and negligence can be crimes as well as ethical failures.

## Part 4 — Social Change

- **Video for this topic:** [Theories of Social Change](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTUFXCZzT7E) — Kaizer Mehta.
- **Social change:** transformation in social structures, institutions, culture, and behavior over time.
- **Causes:**
  - Technological innovation
  - Economic change
  - Political change and policy
  - Cultural change and ideas
  - Demographic change
  - Environmental change
  - Social movements and conflict
- **Theories of social change:**
  - **Evolutionary theory:** gradual development from simple to complex.
  - **Conflict theory:** change results from conflict between groups with unequal power.
  - **Functionalist theory:** change responds to needs and maintains equilibrium.
  - **Cyclical theory:** societies rise and decline in cycles.
- **Modernization:** transition toward industrial, urban, and technologically advanced societies.
- **Globalization:** increasing interconnection of economies, cultures, and institutions.
- **Effects:** new opportunities, inequality, cultural homogenization, resistance, environmental pressure.
- **Engineering relevance:** technology is a major driver of social change; engineers should consider intended and unintended social consequences.
