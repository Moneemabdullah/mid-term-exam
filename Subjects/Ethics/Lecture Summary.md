## Slide 1 — Introduction to Engineering Ethics

- **Video for this topic:** [Engineering Ethics: Crash Course Engineering #27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5KZx81crb48) — CrashCourse.

### Key definitions

- **Ethics:** principles or values that help determine right and wrong in human behavior.
- **Engineering ethics:** the study of decisions, policies, and values that are morally desirable in engineering practice and research.
- **Morals:** principles or habits about right and wrong in one’s own conduct; personal, not imposed by an external authority. From Latin *mos*, meaning custom.

### Ethics vs morals

| Ethics | Morals |
|---|---|
| Professional and public | Personal and internal |
| Based on reasoned principles and codes | Based on upbringing, culture, conscience |
| Applies to role responsibilities | Applies to personal conduct |
| Justified publicly | Often felt intuitively |

### Why engineering ethics matters

1. Engineering decisions affect many people.
2. Engineers have a duty to protect public safety.
3. Ethical behavior builds trust between engineers, organizations, and society.
4. Modern technology can affect large populations, so small mistakes can have big impacts.
5. Decisions can have long-term social and environmental consequences.

### What ethics is not

- **Not law:** legal does not always mean ethical; ethical does not always mean legal.
- **Not personal opinion:** “I feel this is wrong” is not a reasoned ethical argument.
- **Not religion:** professional ethics must work across diverse beliefs.
- **Not cultural norms:** “everyone does it” does not make it ethical.

### Scope of engineering ethics

Engineering ethics applies across the whole lifecycle:

1. Concept and planning
2. Design and testing
3. Production and deployment
4. Use and maintenance
5. Retirement and environmental impact

### Skills for ethical reasoning

- **Moral reasonableness:** willingness to be fair and reasonable.
- **Respect for persons:** genuine concern for everyone involved.
- **Tolerance of diversity:** accept different moral and cultural perspectives.
- **Moral hope:** resolve conflicts through rational, evidence-based dialogue.
- **Integrity:** honesty, strong principles, and consistency.

### Moral issues

- **Moral issue:** an issue with the potential to help or harm anyone, including oneself.
- **Micro-ethics:** everyday problems in engineering practice.
- **Macro-ethics:** broader social problems, sometimes at regional or national level.

## Slide 2 — Ethics in Organization

- **Video for this topic:** [Workplace Rights and Responsibilities](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g7rq1DSvR7g) — NPTEL IIT Kharagpur.

### Types of inquiries

| Type | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Normative** | What ought to be done? | “We ought to keep our promises.” |
| **Conceptual** | What does the concept mean? | “What is safety?” |
| **Factual/descriptive** | What are the facts? | “What are the effects of polluted water?” |

### Personal vs professional ethics

- **Personal ethics:** how you conduct yourself in private life.
- **Professional ethics:** additional obligations that come with being an engineer:
  - Duty to public safety, even over employer interests
  - Competence in your field
  - Honest representation of qualifications and work
  - Protection of confidential information
  - Fair treatment of colleagues and subordinates

### Principles of ethics

1. Paramount duty to public safety
2. Professional competence
3. Honesty and integrity
4. Fairness and non-discrimination
5. Avoidance of conflicts of interest
6. Confidentiality
7. Credit and recognition
8. Environmental responsibility
9. Professional development of others

### Workplace ethics

- **Definition:** values, moral principles, and standards followed by employers and employees in the workplace.
- It shapes employee–employee and employee–customer relationships.
- It may be written or unwritten, but it is meant to be followed.

### Employee rights

- Equal work opportunities
- Fair workdays and work hours
- Weekly rest day
- Fair wages and benefits
- Security of tenure
- Direct payment of wages
- Minimum employment age
- Safe working conditions
- Rights to self-organization and collective bargaining

### Unethical behavior at work

Examples: lies, bribery, taking credit for others’ work, unpaid overtime, verbal or physical harassment, violence, undue pressure, nepotism, theft, embezzlement, corrupt practices, unrealistic expectations.

### How to address unethical behavior

1. Have clear rules.
2. Accept feedback and complaints.
3. List consequences for unethical behavior.
4. Apply swift justice and disciplinary action.

### Employer–employee obligations

**Employers owe employees:** safe workplace, fair compensation, respect and fair treatment, compliance with employment laws, development opportunities, clear communication, privacy, reasonable accommodations, ethical business practices, necessary tools.

**Employees owe employers:** quality work, adherence to policies, punctuality, honesty and integrity, respect and cooperation, proactive learning, confidentiality, responsible use of resources, constructive feedback, loyalty and positive representation.

### Why workplace ethics matter

- Encourages positive employee behavior.
- Creates a healthy work environment.
- Strengthens employee–supervisor relationships.
- Boosts productivity and job satisfaction.
- Poor ethics damages stakeholder relationships and reputation.

### Obligations to clients

The NSPE code emphasizes fairness, loyalty, fidelity to public needs, personal honor, professional integrity, current knowledge, and competence.

## Slide 3 — NSPE Code of Ethics

- **Video for this topic:** [NSPE Code of Ethics for Engineers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=keULx2WSr6A) — joe joe.

### Fundamental canons

Engineers shall:

1. Hold paramount the safety, health, and welfare of the public.
2. Perform services only in areas of their competence.
3. Issue public statements only in an objective and truthful manner.
4. Act for each employer or client as faithful agents or trustees.
5. Avoid deceptive acts.
6. Conduct themselves honorably, responsibly, ethically, and lawfully to enhance the profession.

### Rules of practice

- Public safety comes first.
- Work only within competence.
- Make objective and truthful public statements.
- Act as a faithful agent or trustee.
- Avoid deceptive acts.
- Disclose conflicts of interest.
- Do not accept improper compensation.
- Do not falsify qualifications.
- Do not offer or accept bribes.

### Professional obligations

- Be honest and integrity-driven.
- Serve the public interest.
- Avoid deception.
- Protect confidentiality.
- Avoid conflicts of interest.
- Do not harm other engineers’ reputations unfairly.
- Accept personal responsibility.
- Continue professional development.
- Support sustainable development.

## Quick revision sheet

- Ethics = principles; morals = personal conduct.
- Engineering ethics = professional decisions affecting public safety and trust.
- Five scope stages: concept → design → production → use → retirement.
- Five reasoning skills: reasonableness, respect, tolerance, hope, integrity.
- Three inquiry types: normative, conceptual, factual/descriptive.
- Workplace ethics = employer + employee standards.
- NSPE first canon: public safety, health, and welfare.
- NSPE six canons: safety, competence, truthful statements, faithful agent, no deception, honorable conduct.
